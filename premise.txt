

OVERALL GOAL:
a pixel art game (1-4 players, cooperative if multiplayer) game that can run comfortably on a GeForce 940MX which in its essence 
is the amalgamation of Stardew Valley, Graveyard Keeper, with elements of World of Warcraft. 
meaning, it should be a farming-sim style game (WITHOUT TOO MUCH TEDIUM as these games can 
sometimes lean into) that is comfortable to play (meaning, without a constant din of tension 
and anxiety in gameplay elements, although tension is obviously important in the context of 
combat and some social interactions). the premise of the game is that the hero (or heroes) 
have crash landed their spacecraft on a planet where magic is real, but the technology level 
is about the same as the 1920s america (there are automobiles, telephones, radios, airplanes, airships) 
BUT this is ***NOT*** a steampunk themed game (DO NOT use ANY steampunk themes). it is a 
town-within-agrarian-surroundings sort of society. the ship the heroes crashed in is 
damaged but remains hidden due to the cloaking device being powered by the ambient energy of 
the planet's gravity well, but it is otherwise unable to leave. near to the crashsite is a 
town full of interesting NPC characters of various races (human, elven, dwarven, halfling), 
with quests to fulfill. the town is on the frontier of a kingdom that is under the threat of 
attack from the evil opposing kingdom comprised of orcs, goblins, trolls, and darkelves. an 
important moral lesson of the game is that kingdoms can be at war, but the individual people 
of the kingdoms are complex individual human beings that aren't always aligned with the whims 
of their governing states.

THE MOST IMPORTANT THING ABOUT THE STORY:
just like in real life, despite the tension between the kingdoms, life has to go on. there are 
stories of border skirmishes among the armies, but merchants from either side still travel 
freely between the kingdoms, pilgrims still visit faraway shrines across the border, and people 
still travel back across the border to visit home for the festivals. people still have to work 
to earn a living. the main storyline does center around the escalating tensions between the 
two kingdoms, but that is always just something in the background, except during 
main-storyline-focused game events, as described below. players are free to farm, collect 
resources, go on quests for townspeople, learn the magic that exists on this planet and create 
powerful artifacts, become a traveling merchant, or even join up with the border patrols for 
some extra cash and combat experience. 

THE MAIN STORYLINE:
the player(s) crash on the planet some distance away from the town, and are able to cloak their 
ship to hide their presence from the locals. the Prime Directive dictates that until rescue 
arrives, the crew should try to hide from any local intelligent aliens as much as possible. but 
the player(s) were under attack from an enemy ship, and in order to save their own lives, they
had to hide their ship and leave it behind and seek assistance from the local alien population. 
the game uses the "universal translator" trope. tropes are fine in this game. we're just trying 
to have fun and be comfortable. so, the hero(es) set off to the nearest town and finds an 
abandoned farm that they take up residence in. they are discovered by a neighbor, and there is 
a slightly tense interaction where the neighbor isn't sure about the intenetions of the heroes, 
but he agrees that the party can stay at the farm until the next day, when he will bring the 
mayor of the town to meet them. the party makes up a story about being from very far away and 
looking for a new place to settle. everything goes fine, bc the mayor is always looking for 
new people to help out the local economy, but one of the sidekicks of the mayor mentions 
something about the party being spies, which then triggers the whole storyline for the players 
about the tense situation with the next kingdom over. anyway, there are 52 townspeople and 
farmers and various NPCs the players can meet. they are all varied in their positions in 
town, employment, social standing, everything like that. there are some archetypes, but mostly 
just think of a peaceful town with mostly peaceful townsfolk and farmers. the players get set 
up and begin farming, can explore, etc. the seasons are like they are here on earth, but magic 
is definitely real: farmers hire the local magician to cast spells on their fields just as they 
hire the local pilot to spray their fields with insecticides. the world is fairly sparsely 
populated, so there isn't a huge crush of people coming and going. generally everyone knows 
everyone else in town. there's a town drunk, there's a town bum, there's a town magician, there 
is a town pilot, there is a radio station in the town. there's also a military base with 4-5 
soldiers in garrison. after the first year goes by in the town, suddenly there is some news on 
the radio that a new star has appeared in the sky, and it's growing ever so slightly brighter 
every day. this "star" is the enemy ship that has tracked the players to the planet, and it's 
coming to kill the party. the party can't use their technology to scan the ship or they will 
be discovered, so there is some side quest about getting together with the town's Radio Club 
(think computer club before there were computesr) to try and set up some radar to scan the 
"star". anyway, the authorities of both kingdoms believe it's a bad omen of things to come, 
and tensions start to escalate further because of it. one of the key elements of the story arc 
is that the chief engineer of the crashed starship is an NPC that is actually a member of the 
party, because they have chosen to stay behind to keep the ship's cloak running. but when the 
enemy ship enters the story, the players will be given a lot of quests to go back and forth 
to the crashed ship to deliver e.g. sulfur or copper or zinc or iron or whatever, in order to 
supply the chief engineer with the things he needs to try and fix the ship to call for rescue.

we will get to the end of the story eventually, but i think this is enough for you to get 
started.

some important gameplay considerations:

COMBAT:

combat is realtime, and similar to how combat is played in World of Warcraft, or diablo, with 
individual inventories and fast-use slots for things like potions or spells or handguns or 
swords or whatever. the players gain experience and levels and whatnot. any questions, ask me.

FARMING:

more or less just like stardew valley or graveyard keeper or something: simplistic, fun, easy 
farming system with a lot of things to do with the harvest, like cooking recipes and selling 
it on the market and using it in quests and things. any questions, ask me.

MINING:

semi-dangerous mines with enemies, but there is a difference between mining areas and enemy 
areas, meaning: some places to mine ore have no enemies, and some places to mine are overrun.
any questions, ask me.

FISHING:

every game has a fishing system, and this one is no different. lots of seasonal and different 
kinds of fish with different difficulties, and different kinds of "ingredients" they provide 
for things like spellcasting and food. any questions, ask me.

MAGIC:

it's a system of magic more like diablo or WoW, which is way more involved than the magic 
systems you see in stardew valley or graveyard keeper. it's fairly typical magic system, with 
spells costing mana, etc. there are scrolls and spellbooks and things. any questions, ask me.

IMPORTANT NOTES:

any questions, ask me. you are going to be driving most of the way here, but any big design 
decisions, just ask me. for small stuff, or things that seem very natural (magic users have 
a penalty to casting spells if they are wearing heavy metal armore, this kind of thing), 
then you can just go ahead with the most natural decision, and then tell me in the regular 
summaries before commits. you will commit of your own accord and NEVER EVER push the commits 
to remote repositories without my EXPLICIT say-so.
